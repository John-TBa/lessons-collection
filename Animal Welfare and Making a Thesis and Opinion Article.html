Part 2: Making a Thesis and Opinion Article

Concepts That You Need to Understand Before The Lesson:

  • Evidence (Fact) and Claim (Opinion)

  1. Facts (Evidence)

    • These are statements that can be proven or disproven (False Facts).
    • The accuracy of the info. can be checked through research, observations, and other forms of data retrieval.
    • Examples: The periodic table has 118 Elements. (Proven Fact) | The periodic table has 19 groups of elements. (False/Disproven Fact)

  2. Claims (Opinions)

    • These are statements that are made based on personal beliefs or culture. (Subjective)
    • The accuracy of the info. cannot be proven through research. An opinion is still an opinion no matter how much you believe in it.
    • Examples: A red moon signifies that an apocalypse is coming. (Opinion) | I believe that black tea is better than green tea because it offers more caffeine. (Informed Opinion)

Making a Thesis

  • A thesis has 3 parts: the context (Material that you are reacting on), interpretation (Your main claim in relation to the material), and the connection (the verb that links the connection between your interpretation and the context).

  • Example: Zaldarriaga's article on animal welfare effectively addressed the issue of public dilemma on strenghening anti-animal abuse programs.

Finding Main Ideas to be Used in Your Article

  • Look for at least 4 verbs or nouns in your thesis that could be further discussed in your article.

  • Zaldarriaga's article on animal welfare effectively addressed the issue of public dilemma on strenghening anti-animal abuse programs.

  • Note that every paragraph (main idea) needs to have evidence.

Making an Opinion Article

  • An opinion article is divided into 3 parts: the introduction, body, and conclusion.

  1. Introduction
    • Hook - Grab the attention of your reader/s

    • Thesis

    • Brief Summary of the material that you are making an opinion on

  2. Body
    • Main Idea 1/Statement of Opinion
    • Evidence that supports your idea (from different sources)
    • Opinion based on the material's claim

    • Main Idea 2/Statement of Opinion
    • Evidence that supports your idea (from different sources)

    • Opinion based on the material's claim

    • Main Idea 3/Statement of Opinion

    • Evidence that supports your idea (from different sources)

    • Opinion based on the material's claim
