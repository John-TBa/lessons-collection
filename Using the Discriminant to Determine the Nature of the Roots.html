Determining the Nature of the Roots

What is a Discriminant?

  • It determines the nature of the roots of a quadratic equation
  • It is taken from the quadratic formula
  • Represented by a capital letter D
  • D = b2 - 4ac

The Different Natures of Roots:

D > 0 (All Positive Numbers) D = 0 D < 0 (All Negative Numbers)
2 real and unequal roots 2 real and equal roots 2 imaginary and unequal roots/No real roots

Examples:

  • 3x2 + 9x + 2 = 0

  1. D = (9)2 - 4(3)(2)
  2. D = 81 - 24
  3. D = 57
  4. Nature of the Roots: 2 real and unequal roots

  • 2x2 - 5x + 5 = 0

  1. D = (-5)2 - 4(2)(5)
  2. D = 25 - 40
  3. D = -15
  4. Nature of the Roots: No real roots

  • x2 + 4x + 4 = 0

  1. D = (4)2 - 4(1)(4)
  2. D = 16 - 16
  3. D = 0
  4. Nature of the Roots: 2 real and equal roots
