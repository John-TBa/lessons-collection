Part 2: Making a Thesis and Opinion Article

Concepts That You Need to Understand Before The Lesson:

  • Evidence (Fact) and Claim (Opinion)

  1. Facts (Evidence)

    • These are statements that can be proven or disproven (False Facts).
    • The accuracy of the info. can be checked through research, observations, and other forms of data retrieval.
    • Examples: The periodic table has 118 Elements. (Proven Fact) | The periodic table has 19 groups of elements. (False/Disproven Fact)

  2. Claims (Opinions)

    • These are statements that are made based on personal beliefs or culture. (Subjective)
    • The accuracy of the info. cannot be proven through research. An opinion is still an opinion no matter how much you believe in it.
    • Examples: A red moon signifies that an apocalypse is coming. (Opinion) | I believe that black tea is better than green tea because it offers more caffeine. (Informed Opinion)

Making a Thesis

  • A thesis has 3 parts: The context (Material that you are reacting on), interpretation (Your main claim in relation to the material), and the connection (the li between your interpretation and the context).

  • Example: Zaldarriaga's article on animal welfare effectively addressed the issue of public dilemma on strenghening anti-animal abuse programs.
