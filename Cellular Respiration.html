Cellular Respiration

General Equation: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 O2 + 36-38 ATP

Defining Cellular Respiration

  • It is a metabolic pathway/process that occurs in the mitochondria and occurs in all organisms
  • It involves the breaking down of glucose
  • It produces energy (ATP) for cells
  • There are 2 types of cellular respiration, aerobic which requires oxygen, and anaerobic which does not require oxygen

How does the process happen?

  • The body has little "messengers" called hormones that send signals to the body's cells to initiate the process of cellular respiration. There are 3 important things that the hormones need to do before the process starts.

  1. Increase nutrient uptake
  2. Activate metabolic enzymes
  3. Produce more mitochondria

Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic Cellular Respiration has 3 main processes:
  1. Glycolysis
  2. Krebs Cycle/Citric Acid Cycle
  3. Electron Transport Chain (w/ Chemiosmosis)

1. Glycolysis

  • This process basically involves the breaking down of a glucose molecule into smaller molecules (Pyruvate Molecules)
  • It is an anaerobic process that occurs in the cell's cytoplasm (outside the mitochondria)
  • After glycolysis, cellular respiration could undergo aerobic respiration (Krebs Cycle), or undergo anaerobic respiration (Fermentation)

  • Equation: C6H12O6 + 2 ATP + 2 NAD+ → 2 C3 H6 O3 (C3 when simplified) + 4 ATP + 2 NADH

  • Reactants: C6H12O6 + 2 ATP + 2 NAD+

  • Products: 2 C3 + 4 ATP + 2 NADH

1.5. Intermediate Process/C3 Oxidation

  • This process basically just makes Acetyl CoA which is needed for the Krebs Cycle
  • It is an aerobic process that occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria

  • Equation: 2 C3 + 2 CoA + 2 NAD+ → 2 CO2 + 2 Acetyl CoA + 2 NADH

  • Reactants: 2 C3 + 2 CoA + 2 NAD+

  • Products: 2 CO2 + 2 Acetyl CoA + 2 NADH

2. The Krebs Cycle

  • This process produces the most carbon dioxide and breaks down most of the molecules
  • It is an aerobic process that occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria
  • Equation: 2 Acetyl CoA + (2 ADP + Pi) + 6 NAD+ + 2 FAD+ → 4 CO2 + 2 ATP + 6 NADH + 2 FADH2

  • Reactants: 2 Acetyl CoA + (2 ADP + Pi) + 6 NAD+ + 2 FAD+

  • Products: 4 CO2 + 2 ATP + 6 NADH + 2 FADH2

3. Electron Transport Chain

  • This process produces both water and the most ATP
  • Occurs in the inner membrane of the mitochondria

  • Equation: 6 O2 + NADH (from earlier processes) + FADH2 (from earlier processes) → 6 H2O + 30-32 ATP

  • Reactants: 6 O2 + NADH (from earlier processes) + FADH2 (from earlier processes)

  • Products: 6 H2O + 30-32 ATP

  • The general equation C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 O2 + 36-38 ATP is basically just the summary of the process

What exactly is Chemiosmosis?

  • Chemiosmosis is basically the partner process of the ETC that produces ATP through the ATP synthase
  • The ATP synthase is an enzyme found in the inner membrane which adds a phosphate group to ADP to make it into ATP (Not to be confused with the term "coenzyme" as those are molecules like the FADH2 and the NADH which help transport electrons)
  • In a very simple explanation, the ETC's electrons basically push positive ions (protons) into the intermembrane space to produce a proton gradient. This proton gradient then runs down their electrochemical gradient in the intermembrane space which leads into the ATP synthase. The proton gradient powers the ATP synthase, allowing it to produce ATP.

Anaerobic Respiration

Anaerobic Respiration only has 1 process, it is called Fermentation.
Fermentation has 2 main types: Alcoholic (Occurs in yeast cells) and Lactic Acid (Occurs in muscle cells)

  • It does not require oxygen
  • Occurs in the cytoplasm
  • Equation for Lactic Acid Fermentation: C3 + NADH → Lactic Acid (Lactate) + 2 ATP + NAD+
  • Equation for Alcoholic Fermentation: C3 + NADH → C2 H5 OH (Alcohol/Ethanol) + 2 ATP + CO2 + NAD+

Extra Information about Hormone Types

  1. Thyroid Hormone - Regulator of mitochondria production
  2. Insulin Hormone - Allows sugar (glucose) to enter cells (exit bloodstream)
  3. Adrenaline/Epinephrine Hormone - Signals the body to break down glycogen into glucose
  4. Glucagon Hormone - Signals the liver to release glucose
  5. Ghrelin Hormone - Hunger hormone; basically signals the body that it needs to eat

What is ATP used for?

ATP is basically used for all bodily functions.
