Endosymbiotic Theory

What is it exactly?

Endosymbiotic Theory, from the root words "endo" and "symbiotic," is a theory that states that in the ancient times,
prokaryotic cells merged with bacteria (Has DNA) to form a symbiotic relationship, specifically mutualism where the bacteria lives inside the cell (Both organisms benefit).
The end result of such after a long time are new eukaryotic cells.

What were the things formed?

As a result of endosymbiosis, the 2 cell organelles, the mitochondria and chloroplast, came to be.
The mitochondria originated from a form of aerobic bacteria which ended up living inside an ancient prokaryotic cell, turning into aerobes which were used by the cell for cellular respiration,
and then eventually into the organelle that we know today.
The chloroplast underwent a similar process, but instead of aerobic bacteria, it was cyanobacteria. This cyanobacteria allowed the cell that it lived in to produce energy through photosynthesis

What are these organelles used for?

To put it in simple terms, chloroplasts are used for photosynthesis (The process that creates stored energy through organic material that can be converted to usable energy).
These are found commonly in plants, but they are found in all photoautotrophs (Organisms that make their own "food" through photosynthesis).
The mitochondria, on the other hand, is used for cellular respiration (The process that produces energy). This can be found in all organisms.

The "General" Equations of Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

Photosynthesis:
6 CO2 + 12 H2O + Light = C6 H12 O6 + 6 O2

Cellular Respiration:
C6H12O6 + 6 O 2 = 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + Energy (ATP)

The left side of the equation contains the reactants (The stuff that is absorbed), while the right side contains the products (What the process makes).
